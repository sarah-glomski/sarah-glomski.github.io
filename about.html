About me

Design is the common thread.

Hi there, I’m Sarah! I’m a Mechanical Engineering PhD student at Stanford and an EDGE Doctoral Fellow. I build robot learning pipelines, do systems engineering with haptics, and teach design as a course assistant for ME310, Stanford’s project-based design course. Whether I’m training a robot to work alongside people or helping students prototype, the common thread is design.

Before Stanford, I spent five years at Duke, earning a B.S.E. in Biomedical Engineering (Magna Cum Laude with Departmental Distinction) and an M.Eng. in Mechanical Engineering with a certificate in Medical Robotics. Along the way I tracked surgeon performance in Duke’s Surgical Education and Activities Lab, developed a new CT calibration method at Los Alamos National Laboratory, revived a blood draw device as a product development intern at Gener8, and trained models to detect sub-concussive head impacts in Duke’s Injury Biomechanics Lab.

One of my favorite parts of Duke was founding and leading the Brain Injury Awareness Club (BrainIAC), which spread awareness about brain injuries in the Durham community and facilitated support groups for brain injury survivors across North Carolina.

In my free time, I like to try new recipes, take my Tacoma off-roading and camping, and capture nature videos with my drone. For more on my background, take a look at my resume or connect with me on LinkedIn.
